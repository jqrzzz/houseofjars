# Dragons Glow: the official website

*Vang Vieng, Laos · Research and plan, 10 October 2026 · Nothing built yet*

This is the plan for the show's own official website. Start here. The evidence behind it, with sources, is in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md).

---

## The short version

**Why the scam works.** Search for the show today and you find nothing official: no website, no Google Maps listing, nothing on Klook, Trip.com or GetYourGuide. We searched in English, Chinese, Korean, Thai and Lao and found no trace of the real show. When a tourist searches, whatever *looks* official wins. Right now, that is the scammer.

**What to do.** Fill that empty space fast, then make it beautiful. Three steps:

1. **This week, no website needed.** Report the scam site, claim the show on Google Maps, put up "Official tickets only at …" signs at the door and at partner hostels, and start the trademark.
2. **In 2–3 weeks: the official site, version 1.** One fast site in English, Chinese, Korean, Thai and Lao. It says "Official" everywhere and shows prices, nights and how to book. Anyone can check whether a ticket is real. Guests reserve online and pay by Lao QR code or at the door.
3. **Over the next 1–3 months: sell everywhere.** Card, Alipay and WeChat payment through a Lao bank. Listings on Klook, Trip.com, GetYourGuide and Viator. Seller codes for hostels and tour desks. An AI helper that answers guests in their own language.

**The creative idea.** Present the show as *Laos welcoming the dragon*, not "a Chinese show in Laos". In April 2026, officials took red lanterns down at Tham Chang cave after locals called them "un-Lao". A show that honours the Lao Naga beside the Chinese dragon can win over locals and visitors alike.

**What we need from you.** The five answers in [section 10](#10-questions-for-you). Answer more if you have time.

> **About the research.** This cloud workspace could search the web but not open web pages, so every finding comes from search results, not full pages. Facebook, TikTok, WeChat and Xiaohongshu are barely searchable, which is probably where the scam lives. Details are in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md#how-this-research-was-done).

## 1. What we learned

- **The show is invisible online.** No site, listings or reviews turned up in any of the five languages. Three look-alike domains are already registered by someone (`dragonsglow.com`, `dragonglow.com`, `dragonsglow.live`); we couldn't see what is on them.
- **Vang Vieng has no ticketed dinner show.** Evenings mean free fire-spinners by the river, the night market and bars. The closest thing is a hotpot dinner with singing at Hometown of Dragon Hotel, by the train station. The space is open.
- **Who comes.** Laos had 2.6 million visitors in the first half of 2026: 32% Thai, 26% Chinese and 23% Vietnamese. Koreans are fewer, and falling, but they cluster in Vang Vieng. The train brings Chinese visitors from Kunming and day-trippers from Vientiane, an hour away. Lao visitors are a real market too.
- **What the best show websites do:**
  - One price table, and clear packages (show only → dinner and show → VIP).
  - Nights, times and closed days in one line.
  - "Official" in every page title, in every language.
  - A list of authorised sellers.
  - Plain rules for children, dress code, rain and refunds.
- **Where they fail:**
  - Facts that disagree between pages.
  - Old mobile sites, and English only.
  - When the official site is weak, resellers and look-alikes take over. The Chiang Mai lantern festival is the warning: it has no strong official site, so every seller calls itself "official" and tourists can't tell who is real.
- **Payments.** Stripe doesn't work for a Lao company, and a Lao PayPal account most likely can't receive money. What does work:
  - **LaoQR codes.** Thai, Vietnamese and Chinese visitors can pay them from their home banking apps.
  - **Lao bank payment systems** (BCEL, JDB) for cards, Alipay and WeChat Pay.
  - **The big booking sites,** which take the tourist's money and pay you later.
- **You already have half of it.** Shadow Check-in, the House of Jars booking system, already handles booking requests, online bank payments and QR payments. Shadow, the AI concierge, already answers guests. Both could work for the show.

## 2. What the website must do

Seven jobs, most important first.

1. **Prove it's the real one.**
   - "Official website" in every page title and on every page.
   - One web address, printed on every sign, ticket and social profile.
   - A page listing the official channels and authorised sellers.
   - A "Check your ticket" box.
   - The name of the business that receives the money, and the line "We never ask you to pay into a personal account."
2. **Answer six questions in five seconds:** what is it, when, where, how long, how much, how do I book.
3. **Book in under two minutes on a phone,** even on a weak signal.
   - A light page, under about 1 MB.
   - No Google Maps, Google Fonts or YouTube. Chinese visitors on home SIM cards can't reach Google.
4. **Speak the guest's language,** written or checked by people, not machine-translated: English, 简体中文, 한국어, ไทย and ລາວ. Vietnamese and Japanese can come later.
5. **Take money the way tourists can actually pay** (see [section 5](#5-payments)).
6. **Make the night irresistible:** real video and photos, the story, the evening hour by hour, and real reviews.
7. **Help partners sell the real thing:** seller codes for hostels and tour desks, and a partner kit with photos, prices and booking links.

| Page | What's on it |

| **Our story** | The show, the Lao and Chinese team behind it, the Naga and the dragon. |

Every page comes in all five languages.

## 4. Packages

Most leading shows sell a ladder like this. The prices are yours to set. The figures here show only what is normal in the region.

| Package | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Show** | The show and a drink. Friendly to backpacker budgets. |
| **Dinner & Show** ★ *most popular* | Dinner, the show and good seats. |
| **Premium** | The best seats, a better dinner and hotel pickup. |
| **VIP / Private** | A lounge, a welcome drink, a photo with the performers and the front row. |

**For comparison:**
- **Dinner and show in the region:** usually US$35–70.
- **Luang Prabang's *Buddha's Light* show:** US$37–80.
- **The Royal Ballet in Luang Prabang:** 100,000–150,000 kip.
- **A normal dinner in Vang Vieng:** under US$10.

**Ideas worth copying from other shows:**
- Children priced by height, not age.
- An online price lower than the door price.
- Free date changes instead of refunds.
- Dinner guests get the better seats.

## 5. Payments

Start simple, then add more ways to pay.

- **Version 1 (at launch):** guests reserve online and get a QR ticket. They then either:
  - pay at once by Lao QR code (Shadow Check-in's QR payment already does this), or
  - pay at the door, in cash, by LaoQR or by card.

  Thai, Vietnamese and Chinese (UnionPay) visitors can pay a LaoQR code from their own banking apps.
- **Version 2:** a Lao bank's online payment system (BCEL OnePay or JDB) on the website, for Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, Alipay and WeChat Pay.
- **Version 3:** Bókun (from US$49 a month plus 1.5%) lists the show on Klook, Trip.com, GetYourGuide and Viator, with one seat count for every channel. Those sites charge 20–30% per ticket they sell. They also push the scam site down in search results.

**Not possible:** Stripe (it doesn't support Laos) and PayPal (Lao accounts appear able to send money but not receive it).

## 6. Fighting the scam site

Start now, before the website exists.

1. **Keep an evidence log.** For every victim, record:
   - the date and how much they paid;
   - how they paid, and the account or QR name they paid;
   - screenshots and the web address.

   Police, domain companies and Google all act on evidence like this.
2. **Report the site as fraud,** not as a trademark problem (that route is slower). Report it to:
   - the company the domain is registered with, and the site's host;
   - Google Safe Browsing (so Chrome warns visitors) and Microsoft;
   - Meta, if it uses Facebook or Instagram;
   - Google, if it runs ads on the show's name.
3. **Go to the police** with the evidence log: the tourist police, and Laos's anti-online-fraud hotline 1533 (to confirm locally).
4. **File the trademark in Laos now.** Laos is "first to file": whoever registers the name first owns it. It costs about US$85 in government fees plus about US$690 for an agent. Register the English name, the Chinese name and the logo. A trademark also makes takedowns on Google, Meta and domain disputes faster.
5. **Claim the show online:** a Google Maps listing, a verified Facebook page and TripAdvisor, all pointing to one official address.
6. **Put up signs** at the venue and partner hostels: "Official tickets only at dragonsglow.la or at our counter."
7. **Decide your door policy** for scam victims, and state it plainly online. A suggestion: honour real tickets only, but offer a discounted standby seat when there is room, in return for the victim's evidence.

## 7. Creative concepts

Four directions. Pick one, mix two, or tell us what's wrong with all of them.

### A. Two Guardians *(recommended)*
The Naga of the Nam Song river and the Dragon of the sky are cousins, not rivals. Once a night they meet to light up the valley.
- **Mood:** mythic, warm and respectful, building to a big finale.
- **Colours:** night indigo, river teal, Naga-weave white, lantern gold and a touch of vermilion.
- **Why:** Lao first, Chinese spectacle second. Locals feel proud, Chinese guests feel at home, and Westerners get a story.

### B. Lantern Karst
The limestone mountains glow like giant paper lanterns while you eat beneath them.
- **Mood:** romantic and made for photos: Instagram, Xiaohongshu and Korean feeds.
- **Colours:** indigo, amber paper glow (not red, after Tham Chang), limestone white and jade.

### C. Sparks on the Nam Song
Fire, water and showers of molten-iron sparks ("iron flowers", 打铁花, viral on Douyin), reflected in the river.
- **Mood:** a high-energy festival.
- **Colours:** charcoal, molten orange, ember red and river silver.
- **Only if** the show really has fire.

### D. The Dragon's Pearl Supper
Dinner comes first in this premium, calm evening, built around the pearl the dragon chases and the jewel the Naga guards.
- **Mood:** refined, quiet luxury, for couples and bigger spenders.
- **Colours:** pearl white, black lacquer, muted gold and jade.

**Rules for every direction:**
- Treat the Naga as sacred: never comic, never a monster to defeat.
- No Buddha images used as decoration.
- Lao performers and the Lao language stay visible.
- No words that frame Laos as part of China.

## 8. How we'll build it

- **Reuse what works at House of Jars:**
  - the same fast, secure website setup;
  - Shadow Check-in for bookings, payment and QR tickets, if it can handle show nights with a seat limit (to confirm);
  - Shadow, the AI concierge, as a show helper that answers in any language;
  - the "only confirmed facts" rule, so the site never states a price or time that isn't true. Contradictions are the most common failure on competitor sites.
- **Its own home.** The research and plan sit in this repository for now, in `docs/dragons-glow/`. When we start building, I recommend a separate repository, since the show is a separate business and you may hand it to partners.
- **The web address.** `dragonsglow.la` is free and costs US$37 a year. It is short, says "Laos" and matches houseofjars.la. Two backups are also worth buying so nobody else can take them: `dragonsglowshow.com` and `dragonsglowlaos.com`, about US$11 a year each. **Nothing has been bought;** that needs your OK.

## 10. Questions for you

### If you only answer five, answer these

1. **The scam site.** What is its web address, and how did victims pay (bank account, QR name, WeChat)? Send screenshots if you have them.
2. **The night itself.** What happens from arrival to goodbye, and how long does it take? What is in the show (dragon dance, fire, lanterns, acrobats, iron flowers)? What food is served?
3. **Nights, times and seats.** Which nights, what time, how many guests per night? Is there a season or a rain plan?
4. **Prices and packages today.** What do you charge? Do hostels or agents sell for you now, and for what commission?
5. **The name and the domain.** What is the exact spelling ("Dragons Glow" or "Dragon's Glow")? Is there a Chinese, Korean or Lao name, and a logo? And may I buy `dragonsglow.la` plus the two `.com` backups (about US$60 a year)?

### When you have time

6. Where exactly is the show (venue name and Google Maps pin)? Is it connected to Hometown of Dragon Hotel?
7. Who runs it? What is the company name, and is it registered in Laos? The bank, the trademark and the booking sites all need this.
8. Is the show a Lao-Chinese production, or Chinese with Lao staff? Who performs?
9. Which of the four concepts feels right?
10. Can Shadow Check-in take bookings for a second business, and which bank account would receive the money? Does the show already have a BCEL or JDB merchant account, or its own LaoQR code?
11. Do you pick guests up from their hotels? Which areas?
12. Which WhatsApp, WeChat, KakaoTalk, LINE and Facebook accounts does the show use? Who answers them, and when?
13. Do you have good photos and video? If not, we'll plan a shoot.
14. Who can check the Chinese, Korean, Thai and Lao text?
15. What do you do today when a scam victim arrives at the door?
16. What are the rules on children, dress code, alcohol, vegetarian or halal food, wheelchair access and refunds?
