### Languages, in priority order

1. **English:** the shared language, and what the big booking sites use.
2. **Simplified Chinese:** the #2 market, fed by the train, and the natural audience for a dragon show. Write it for Xiaohongshu and Ctrip readers.
3. **Korean:** smaller, but concentrated in Vang Vieng and already buying packages online.
4. **Thai:** the #1 market. Many Lao readers can read it too.
5. **Lao:** for respect, for permits and partners, and for domestic visitors. Needs a native speaker's check.
6. **Later:** Vietnamese, then Japanese.

### Internet on the ground

- **Speed:** median mobile download in Laos was about 43 Mbps by late 2025 ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-laos)).
- **Coverage:** 4G is good in Vang Vieng town, weaker on the river and in the countryside ([Internet Laos](https://shop.internetlaos.com/blog/is-there-4g-in-vang-vieng-internet-and-esim-guide)).
- **For the site:** keep pages light, use compressed video with still posters, and make booking survive a dropped connection.

### What the best sites share

1. One price table, with adult and child prices and every tier on one screen.
2. A ladder: show → show and dinner (marked "recommended") → better seats → VIP.
3. Pickup included in the middle tiers, with named zones and a same-day cutoff.
4. Schedule as days, time and closed day: "Nightly 20:00, except Tuesday."
5. Children priced by height, which avoids arguments at the gate.
6. Native-language pages with native page titles.
7. "Official" in every title, in every language.
8. A practical FAQ: dress code, children, arrival time, rain, cancellations, transport.
9. Clear booking steps ending in an e-ticket, with WhatsApp, Facebook or phone as backups.
10. Bundles with partners: a hotel, a dinner, a second attraction.
11. A reason to book early online: a lower online price, or an early-bird discount.
12. Proof: awards, press, real photos and video.
13. A kit for agents, so hostels and tour desks sell the real thing.

### The most common failures

- Facts that disagree between pages: show length, refund rules, phone numbers.
- The official site losing in search to resellers.
- One language only.
- Old systems: separate mobile sites, stale event pages, plain http.
- Payment methods not spelled out. No official site found names Alipay or WeChat Pay.

## 5. Payments and booking systems

### What doesn't work from Laos

- **Stripe:** Laos is not a supported country ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/global)). The only route is a company in a supported country such as Thailand or Singapore, which needs legal and tax advice.
- **PayPal:** Lao accounts appear to be send-only, unable to receive or withdraw money ([PayPal community](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Adding-and-updating-info/Laos-PayPal-Receive-and-Withdraw-Laos-Accounts-Banks/td-p/1278836)) [uncertain]. Platforms that pay out only through Stripe or PayPal are therefore blocked too: FareHarbor, Ticket Tailor, Eventbrite.

### What does work

- **BCEL e-commerce / OnePay:**
  - Online gateway for Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Amex, UnionPay and BCEL OnePay QR ([BCEL merchant form](https://www.bcel.com.la/bcel/bcelweb-datas/file-path/forms/en/bcel_web_file_51_8443799135be07aae9e569a344e7b601.pdf)).
  - Alipay and WeChat Pay through partners ([Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/press-releases/bcel-and-wallyt-partner-to-enable-alipay-and-wechat-pay-acceptance-in-laos)) [dated].
  - Fees and set-up time: ask BCEL.
- **JDB:** dynamic LaoQR, Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, WeChat Pay and Alipay ([Vientiane Times](https://vientianetimes.org.la/freefreenews/freecontent_128_Joint_y24.php)) [article undated].
- **LDB:** a QR payment API for online shops ([LDB QR package](https://pub.dev/documentation/ldb_qr_payment/latest/)).
- **Tourists paying LaoQR from home apps:**

**How the big booking sites pay:**
- Viator, GetYourGuide and Klook collect from the tourist and pay you later.
- Commission: GetYourGuide 20–30% ([GetYourGuide](https://getyourguide.supply/join/how-much-does-getyourguide-charge)).
- Viator pays by bank transfer and asks for a US W-8BEN-E tax form ([Viator](https://operatorresources.viator.com/create-business-account-for-your-tour-company/)).
- Klook takes registered companies only.
- **To confirm:** whether each one pays into a Lao bank account.

**Checking tickets at the door:**
- One QR code per booking, scanned by an app that marks it used.
- Use one scanning phone per entrance, or keep the app online: two offline phones can both accept the same ticket.
- One seat limit per night, shared by every channel, so nights can't be oversold.

### The House of Jars system

Shadow Check-in already does much of this for the hostel ([BOOKING_API.md](../BOOKING_API.md)):
- booking requests, with a reference for each guest;

- a status page the guest returns to.

## 6. Fighting the scam site

### Takedown, step by step

1. **Evidence:** screenshots, full web addresses, the bank account, QR name or wallet the scam asks for, and victims' receipts, all with dates.
2. **Find the domain company and the host:** [ICANN Lookup](https://lookup.icann.org). If the site sits behind Cloudflare, use [Cloudflare's abuse form](https://abuse.cloudflare.com); it passes the report to the real host.
3. **Report to the domain company as phishing or fraud.** ICANN's rules require domain companies to act on "DNS abuse" such as phishing, "reasonably and promptly". In 2026 ICANN found one company in breach for a nine-day delay ([ICANN notice](https://www.icann.org/uploads/compliance_notice/attachment/1357/hedlund-to-nestsiarovich-16jul26.pdf)). Trademark complaints are slower and may fall outside those rules.
4. **[Google Safe Browsing](https://safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish/):** if accepted, Chrome and Firefox show a red warning.
5. **[Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/wdsi/support/report-unsafe-site-guest)**, for Edge users.
6. **Google Ads,** if the scam advertises on the show's name: the [counterfeit complaint](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176017).
7. **Meta:** report impersonation. With a trademark, use Meta's brand-protection forms.
8. **Domain dispute (UDRP)** to take the domain over:
   - US$1,500 at WIPO for 1–5 domains ([WIPO fees](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/domain-name-disputes/fees)).
   - A new fast track costs US$4,000 and aims at about one month ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0005)).
   - Needs trademark rights.
9. **Trademark in Laos,** at the Department of Intellectual Property, Ministry of Industry and Commerce ([DIP](https://dip.gov.la/en/?p=5699)):
   - **First to file:** whoever registers first owns the name.
   - **Costs:** about US$85 to register and about US$100 for a search; new rules took effect July 2025 ([Asia IP](https://www.asiaiplaw.com/sector/trademarks/laos-ip-guide-2025)). An agent costs about US$690 ([Kaizen](https://kaizencpa.com/services/info/id/129.html)).
   - **Going abroad:** through the Madrid system, protection can extend to China, Korea and Thailand ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/madrid/en/news/2015/news_0023.html)).
   - **What to file:** probably class 41 (entertainment) and class 43 (food). Confirm with the agent.
10. **Police:**
    - Laos's anti-online-fraud hotline **1533** ([KPL](https://kpl.gov.la/detail.aspx?id=96241)) [launch year unclear; confirm].
    - Tourist police in Vientiane: 021 251 128 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/laos/getting-help)).
    - Take the evidence file to Vang Vieng police in person.

### Lessons from famous attractions

- **Taj Mahal, April 2026:** the government named the fake site and listed its official booking addresses; the national cybercrime centre then warned too ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/taj-mahal-ticket-scam-asi-warns-tourists-against-fake-booking-websites-enn26040802243)). **Name the fake site and the real one, publicly.**
- **Anne Frank House:** sells only on its own site and releases tickets on a fixed schedule, so sales outside it are obviously fake ([Anne Frank House](https://annefrank.org/en/museum/tickets/choose-your-ticket/)).
- **Vatican Museums:** names the *only* official ticket site ([Vatican](https://www.museivaticani.va/content/museivaticani/en.html)).
- **Alhambra:** "If the official site is sold out, don't believe others who say they have tickets" ([Granada tourism](https://turismo.granada.org/en?page=1)).
- **Sagrada Família, August 2026:** fake sites copy the official branding and confirmation pages ([Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-gb/blog/hotforsecurity/spanish-authorities-warn-about-fake-ticket-websites)).
- **Machu Picchu:** staff spotted forged tickets at the gate, which led to arrests ([Andina](https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-peru-police-arrest-two-suspected-counterfeiters-of-machu-picchu-entrance-tickets-1084495.aspx)). **Checking tickets at the door works.**

### Getting above the scam in search [unverified: standard practice]

- A Google Business Profile with the website set to the official address.
- Organization and Event structured data on the site, linking every official account (the House of Jars site already does this for the hostel).
- Listings on the big booking sites, which rank well for the show's name.
- Links from Vang Vieng hotels and hostels, and a TripAdvisor listing.
- Search ads on the show's own name in English, Chinese and Korean.
- Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Posts on Xiaohongshu and Naver.

### At the door

- **A policy for scam victims:** you don't have to honour fake tickets. A goodwill option is a discounted or standby seat when there is room, in return for their evidence.
- **A printed sheet in their language:** call your bank, call 1533, contact your embassy.
- **Teach guests to check the payee:** LaoQR apps show the merchant name before paying, so it should say the show's company name.
- **Authorised sellers:** each hostel or tour desk gets a seller code and a window sticker linking to the authorised-sellers page, and earns a commission so the real channel pays better than the fake one.

## 7. Culture: the Naga, the dragon and Vang Vieng

### The April 2026 lantern episode

- **What happened:** on 21 April 2026, Vientiane provincial authorities removed red lanterns from Tham Chang (Chang Cave) in Vang Vieng after public criticism that they looked foreign and "Chinese-style". Some mocked the site as "Tham Chin" (Chinese Cave) ([Laotian Times](https://laotiantimes.com/2026/04/21/red-lanterns-removed-from-vang-viengs-chang-cave-over-un-lao-concerns/)).
- **Why it was ordered:** the Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism directed that the site "comply with Laos' tourism standards".
- **The cave's history:** it sheltered locals from Chinese (Haw) invaders in the 19th century ([Backpackers Wanderlust](https://www.backpackerswanderlust.com/tham-chang-cave-blue-lagoon-two-vang-vieng/)).
- **The wider mood:** visible Chinese business in Vang Vieng is already a local concern ([RFA, 2023](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/laos/chinese-tourism-projects-12142023154112.html)).

**What it means:** a "China-style" show must look and feel welcome in Laos, not imposed on it.

### The Naga

- **A living heritage:** UNESCO listed Lao Naga-motif weaving as living heritage in 2023. The Naga is a river serpent, an ancestor who protects people, and brings floods when angered ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-craft-of-naga-motif-weaving-in-lao-communities-01973)).
- **Cities and temples:** a seven-headed Naga is said to live under That Luang in Vientiane, and Luang Prabang is guarded by 15 Naga ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/this-serpent-god-is-everywhere-in-laos)).
- **Naga fireballs:** lights rise from the Mekong around Ok Phansa, the end of Buddhist Lent ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_fireball)).
- **This month:** Ok Phansa falls around 26 October 2026, and Luang Prabang's festival of lit boats (Lai Heua Fai), with Naga floats, follows [dates uncertain]. It is Laos's own festival of light and Naga, a natural moment for the show's story, if done with respect.

### Naga and dragon: cousins

- **The shared root:** both are serpent-born water and rain beings. When Buddhism reached China, the Naga kings of the Buddhist texts became "dragon kings" ([Onmark](https://onmarkproductions.com/html/dragon.shtml)).
- **The difference:**
  - **The Naga:** a sacred river guardian and Lao ancestor.
  - **The Chinese dragon:** horned and clawed, it rules sky and water and stands for good fortune.
- **Blend them as equals.** Never present the Naga as a "version of" or "smaller than" the dragon. Give the Naga a Lao-led scene: Lao performers, khaen music, and Naga-weave patterns credited to their weavers.

### Chinese light and fire traditions [unverified]

- **The Lantern Festival** (元宵节): around 20 February 2027.
- **The dragon dance** (舞龙).
- **Hong Kong's Tai Hang fire dragon:** a long straw dragon studded with burning incense sticks.
- **Iron flowers** (打铁花): molten iron struck into the night sky in showers of sparks. Viral on Douyin and Xiaohongshu since about 2023. It needs a trained troupe, a large safety zone and fire permits.

### Sensitivities

- **Sovereignty:** no map, slogan or word that suggests Laos belongs to China. Lead with "Vang Vieng, Laos"; name Lao partners and staff; use Lao on signs and the site.
- **Religion:**
  - The Naga is never comic, drunk, sexualised or a monster to be beaten.
  - No Buddha images or monks as decoration.
  - Don't clash with Ok Phansa and temple events.
  - Consult the provincial Information, Culture and Tourism office and local temple elders [unverified].
- **Lao pride:** don't call Naga traditions "Thai".
- **Western readers:**
  - No "exotic Orient" clichés.
  - Be honest that it is a Chinese-style show made with Lao partners. Overclaiming "authentic Lao culture" will be called out in reviews.
  - Stress craft, safety, and sealed or branded drinks.
